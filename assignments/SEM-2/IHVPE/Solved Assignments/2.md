## Section A (2 Marks Each)

### 1. Define Self-enquiry and Self-discovery.

Self-enquiry is the process of examining one's thoughts, beliefs, values, and actions to understand oneself better. Self-discovery is the realization of one's true nature, strengths, weaknesses, and purpose in life through continuous reflection and learning. 

### 2. What are Universal Values?

Universal Values are fundamental principles accepted across cultures and societies. These include truth, honesty, compassion, respect, justice, responsibility, and non-violence. They guide human behavior and promote harmony and well-being. 

### 3. What is meant by Deep Listening?

Deep Listening is the practice of listening attentively and empathetically to understand another person's thoughts, feelings, and perspectives without interruption, judgment, or prejudice. It promotes effective communication and stronger relationships. 

### 4. Define Bias in ethical decision-making.

Bias in ethical decision-making refers to personal prejudices, assumptions, or preferences that influence judgments and decisions unfairly. Bias can prevent objective thinking and lead to unethical or inaccurate conclusions. 

### 5. What is the meaning of Yama and Niyama?

Yama and Niyama are ethical principles described by Patanjali in Yoga philosophy. Yama consists of moral restraints such as non-violence and truthfulness, while Niyama includes personal disciplines such as cleanliness, self-study, and contentment.

# Section B (7 Marks Each)

### 6. Explain the concept of Knowing the Self and its importance in ethical living.

Knowing the Self refers to understanding one's thoughts, emotions, values, strengths, weaknesses, and true nature. It is a process of self-awareness that helps individuals recognize their motivations and behavior patterns. Through self-reflection and introspection, a person develops a deeper understanding of personal goals and responsibilities.

Self-knowledge is essential for ethical living because it helps individuals make conscious and responsible decisions. A person who understands oneself is less likely to be influenced by negative emotions, peer pressure, or selfish interests. Self-awareness promotes honesty, integrity, empathy, and accountability.

Knowing the Self also helps in managing stress, improving relationships, and maintaining emotional balance. It enables individuals to align their actions with moral values and contribute positively to society. Therefore, self-knowledge forms the foundation of ethical behavior and personal growth. 

### 7. Discuss the four profiles of Self-identity (Inner Potential, Social, Professional, Personality).

Self-identity refers to the understanding of who we are and how we perceive ourselves. It can be understood through four important profiles.

**Inner Potential:** This represents an individual's inherent abilities, talents, values, aspirations, and spiritual potential. It focuses on self-awareness and personal growth.

**Social Profile:** This profile relates to an individual's relationships with family, friends, community, and society. It includes social responsibilities, cooperation, and respect for others.

**Professional Profile:** This refers to one's career, skills, competence, work ethics, and professional responsibilities. It reflects how an individual contributes to society through work.

**Personality Profile:** Personality includes attitudes, behavior, communication skills, emotional intelligence, confidence, and character traits that influence interactions with others.

Together, these four dimensions provide a holistic understanding of self-identity and help individuals achieve balanced personal, social, and professional development. 

### 8. Differentiate between Ideology and Values with suitable examples.

Ideology refers to a set of beliefs, ideas, or principles that guide a group, organization, or society toward a specific goal. Values are fundamental standards of right and wrong that influence individual behavior and decision-making.

Values are generally universal and stable, whereas ideologies may differ among groups and can change over time. For example, honesty and compassion are values accepted across cultures. On the other hand, socialism, capitalism, and democracy are examples of ideologies because they represent different systems of political and economic thought.

Values focus on ethical conduct and personal character, while ideologies provide frameworks for social, political, or economic organization. Although ideologies may be based on values, the two concepts are distinct in purpose and application.

# Section C (11 Marks Each)

### 9. Analyze how recognizing assumptions and biases helps in developing ethical behaviour.

Assumptions and biases influence the way people think, judge, and make decisions. Assumptions are beliefs accepted without sufficient evidence, while biases are tendencies that favor certain viewpoints, people, or outcomes. Recognizing these influences is essential for ethical development.

When individuals become aware of their assumptions, they are more likely to question their beliefs and evaluate situations objectively. This reduces misunderstandings and promotes rational decision-making. Similarly, identifying biases helps people avoid unfair treatment, discrimination, and prejudice.

Awareness of assumptions and biases encourages critical thinking, open-mindedness, and respect for diverse perspectives. It enables individuals to listen carefully to others and make decisions based on facts rather than personal preferences. Such awareness strengthens fairness, accountability, and integrity.

In professional environments, recognizing biases helps ensure ethical practices in recruitment, evaluation, leadership, and teamwork. In personal life, it improves relationships and mutual understanding. Therefore, the ability to identify and overcome assumptions and biases is a key step in developing ethical behavior and responsible citizenship. 

### 10. Discuss in detail the importance of Universal Values in personal and professional life.

Universal Values are fundamental principles that guide human behavior regardless of culture, religion, or nationality. These values include honesty, truthfulness, respect, compassion, responsibility, fairness, and integrity. They serve as the foundation of ethical conduct and harmonious living.

In personal life, universal values help individuals build strong character and meaningful relationships. Honesty promotes trust, compassion encourages empathy, and responsibility helps individuals fulfill their duties effectively. These values contribute to emotional well-being, self-respect, and social harmony.

In professional life, universal values play a crucial role in maintaining ethical standards. Integrity and honesty build credibility and trust within organizations. Respect for colleagues promotes teamwork and cooperation. Fairness ensures equal treatment and justice in decision-making. Organizations that follow universal values often experience higher employee satisfaction, better reputation, and long-term success.

Universal values also help resolve conflicts peacefully and promote sustainable development. They encourage individuals to act responsibly toward society and the environment. In an increasingly interconnected world, these values foster mutual understanding and global harmony.

Thus, universal values are essential for personal excellence, professional success, and the creation of a just and peaceful society. 

### 11. Explain the role of Mindfulness and the principles of Yama & Niyama (as given by Patanjali) in developing Human Values and Ethics.

Mindfulness is the practice of being fully aware of one's thoughts, emotions, and actions in the present moment. It helps individuals develop self-control, emotional balance, concentration, and ethical awareness. Mindfulness enables people to respond thoughtfully rather than react impulsively, leading to responsible behavior.

Patanjali's Yoga philosophy includes Yama and Niyama as ethical foundations for personal and social conduct.

**Yama** consists of five moral principles:

1. Ahimsa (Non-violence)
2. Satya (Truthfulness)
3. Asteya (Non-stealing)
4. Brahmacharya (Self-control)
5. Aparigraha (Non-possessiveness)

**Niyama** consists of five personal disciplines:

1. Shaucha (Cleanliness)
2. Santosha (Contentment)
3. Tapas (Discipline)
4. Svadhyaya (Self-study)
5. Ishvara Pranidhana (Surrender to the Divine)

Together, mindfulness, Yama, and Niyama help individuals cultivate honesty, compassion, self-discipline, humility, and responsibility. They promote ethical decision-making, peaceful relationships, and personal growth. These principles remain highly relevant in modern life as they guide individuals toward moral excellence and holistic well-being.
