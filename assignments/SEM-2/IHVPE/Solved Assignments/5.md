## Section A (2 Marks Each)

### 1. Define the concept of Gunas in Indian Philosophy.

In Indian philosophy, Gunas are the three fundamental qualities or energies that influence human behavior, thoughts, and actions. These are Sattva (purity and wisdom), Rajas (activity and desire), and Tamas (ignorance and inertia). The balance of these Gunas shapes an individual's personality and conduct. 

### 2. What are the Antah-karanas?

Antah-karanas are the four inner instruments of the human mind that govern mental and psychological functioning. They are Manas (mind), Buddhi (intellect), Chitta (memory and consciousness), and Ahamkara (ego). Together, they influence perception, decision-making, and personality development. 

### 3. Define Panch Kosha.

Panch Kosha refers to the five sheaths or layers that cover the true self according to Indian philosophy. These are Annamaya Kosha (physical body), Pranamaya Kosha (vital energy), Manomaya Kosha (mind), Vijnanamaya Kosha (intellect), and Anandamaya Kosha (bliss). 

### 4. What is meant by Equanimity?

Equanimity is a state of mental balance, calmness, and stability in which a person remains unaffected by success and failure, pleasure and pain, or favorable and unfavorable situations. It reflects emotional maturity and inner peace. 

### 5. Define Meditated Personality.

A Meditated Personality is a personality characterized by self-awareness, emotional stability, mental clarity, and inner peace developed through meditation, self-discipline, and mindfulness practices.

# Section B (7 Marks Each)

### 6. Explain the three Gunas (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas) with suitable examples.

According to Indian philosophy, all human behavior is influenced by three Gunas: Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas.

**Sattva** represents purity, wisdom, harmony, truth, and balance. Individuals dominated by Sattva tend to be calm, compassionate, disciplined, and knowledgeable. For example, a teacher who works selflessly for the welfare of students reflects Sattvic qualities.

**Rajas** represents activity, ambition, passion, and desire. Rajasic individuals are energetic, goal-oriented, and driven by achievement. For example, an entrepreneur working tirelessly to expand a business demonstrates Rajasic tendencies.

**Tamas** represents ignorance, laziness, confusion, and inactivity. Individuals dominated by Tamas may avoid responsibilities and lack motivation. For example, a person who procrastinates continuously despite having important tasks exhibits Tamasic behavior.

A balanced personality aims to increase Sattva while managing excessive Rajas and reducing Tamas. This balance contributes to personal growth and well-being. 

### 7. Discuss the role of Antah-karanas (Manas, Buddhi, Chitta, Ahamkara) in personality development.

Antah-karanas are the internal faculties that influence human thinking, behavior, and personality.

**Manas (Mind)** receives information from the senses and generates thoughts, emotions, and desires. It plays a key role in perception and emotional responses.

**Buddhi (Intellect)** is responsible for reasoning, analysis, judgment, and decision-making. It helps individuals distinguish between right and wrong.

**Chitta (Memory and Consciousness)** stores experiences, impressions, and memories. It influences habits, attitudes, and behavioral patterns.

**Ahamkara (Ego)** creates the sense of individuality and self-identity. While a healthy ego promotes confidence, excessive ego can lead to pride and conflict.

The balanced functioning of these four components is essential for personality development. They help individuals achieve self-awareness, emotional stability, ethical conduct, and effective decision-making. 

### 8. Explain the concept of Panch Kosha (Five Sheaths) and its importance in understanding human well-being.

The Panch Kosha theory describes human existence as consisting of five layers or sheaths that surround the true self.

1. **Annamaya Kosha** – Physical body nourished by food.
2. **Pranamaya Kosha** – Vital energy responsible for life functions.
3. **Manomaya Kosha** – Mental and emotional layer.
4. **Vijnanamaya Kosha** – Intellectual and wisdom layer.
5. **Anandamaya Kosha** – Blissful and spiritual layer.

This concept emphasizes that human well-being is multidimensional. Physical health alone is not sufficient; mental, emotional, intellectual, and spiritual well-being are equally important.

Understanding Panch Kosha helps individuals adopt a holistic approach to health and personal development. It encourages balanced living through proper nutrition, exercise, emotional management, learning, and spiritual growth, leading to overall happiness and fulfillment.

### 9. Compare Meditated Personality and Agitated Personality in the context of stress management.

A Meditated Personality and an Agitated Personality represent two contrasting approaches to handling stress and life's challenges.

A **Meditated Personality** is characterized by calmness, self-awareness, emotional stability, patience, and clarity of thought. Such individuals are able to manage stress effectively because they respond thoughtfully rather than reacting impulsively. Meditation helps them maintain concentration, emotional control, and inner peace even during difficult situations.

In contrast, an **Agitated Personality** is often restless, anxious, emotionally unstable, and easily disturbed by external circumstances. Such individuals may react impulsively, experience frustration, and find it difficult to cope with stress. Their decision-making ability may be affected by fear, anger, or confusion.

From a stress management perspective, a meditated personality is better equipped to maintain physical and mental well-being. Meditation improves resilience, reduces anxiety, enhances focus, and promotes positive thinking. An agitated personality, on the other hand, may suffer from increased stress, poor relationships, and reduced productivity.

Therefore, cultivating a meditated personality through mindfulness, self-discipline, and regular meditation can significantly improve stress management and overall quality of life. 

### 10. Discuss the concepts of Oneness, Non-duality (Advaita), and Equanimity and their relevance in modern life.

The concepts of Oneness, Non-duality (Advaita), and Equanimity are important principles of Indian philosophy that promote harmony, self-realization, and inner peace.

**Oneness** refers to the interconnectedness of all living beings and the universe. It encourages individuals to view others with compassion, respect, and empathy.

**Advaita (Non-duality)** teaches that the individual self and the universal reality are ultimately one. It emphasizes overcoming feelings of separation and realizing the unity of existence.

**Equanimity** is the ability to remain mentally balanced and calm in both favorable and unfavorable situations. It involves maintaining emotional stability regardless of success or failure.

These concepts are highly relevant in modern life. Oneness promotes social harmony and environmental responsibility. Advaita encourages self-awareness and reduces conflicts arising from ego and discrimination. Equanimity helps individuals manage stress, uncertainty, and emotional challenges effectively.

Together, these principles support personal growth, ethical living, peaceful relationships, and mental well-being. They provide valuable guidance for achieving balance and harmony in today's fast-paced world. 

### 11. Explain the importance of Physical, Mental, Social, and Spiritual Well-being. Also discuss the role of Ayurveda, nutrition, and sports activities in maintaining holistic health.

Holistic health involves the balanced development of physical, mental, social, and spiritual well-being.

**Physical well-being** refers to maintaining a healthy body through proper nutrition, exercise, rest, and healthcare. Good physical health improves energy, productivity, and quality of life.

**Mental well-being** includes emotional stability, positive thinking, stress management, and psychological resilience. It helps individuals cope with challenges effectively.

**Social well-being** involves healthy relationships, cooperation, communication, and active participation in society. Strong social connections contribute to happiness and emotional support.

**Spiritual well-being** relates to inner peace, purpose, values, and self-awareness. It provides meaning and direction in life.

Ayurveda contributes to holistic health by promoting balance between body, mind, and spirit through natural therapies, lifestyle practices, and preventive healthcare. Proper nutrition supplies essential nutrients required for growth, immunity, and overall functioning of the body. Sports and physical activities improve fitness, teamwork, discipline, confidence, and stress management.

Together, Ayurveda, balanced nutrition, and regular sports activities support comprehensive well-being and help individuals lead healthy, productive, and fulfilling lives. Thus, holistic health is essential for personal development and long-term happiness.
