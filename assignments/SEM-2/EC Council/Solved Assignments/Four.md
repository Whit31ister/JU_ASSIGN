# SECTION A

## 1. Define cyberbullying and explain how privacy settings can help prevent it.

Cyberbullying is the use of digital platforms such as social media, messaging applications, emails, or online forums to harass, threaten, embarrass, or intimidate individuals. It can have serious emotional and psychological effects on victims. Privacy settings help prevent cyberbullying by limiting who can view profiles, send messages, comment on posts, or access personal information. By restricting interactions to trusted individuals, users can reduce exposure to potential bullies and online harassment.

---

## 2. What are the warning signs of a social media scam?

Social media scams often contain warning signs that users should recognize. Common indicators include requests for money or personal information, offers that appear too good to be true, suspicious links, and messages creating a sense of urgency. Scammers may also use fake profiles, poor grammar, or impersonate trusted individuals and organizations. Verifying the authenticity of accounts and avoiding unknown links can help users avoid becoming victims of social media scams.

---

## 3. What are the key indicators of a malicious email attachment?

Malicious email attachments often exhibit several warning signs. The email may come from an unknown or suspicious sender, contain unexpected attachments, or use urgent language to encourage immediate action. Attachments with unusual file extensions such as .exe, .bat, or macro-enabled documents can be dangerous. Poor grammar, spelling mistakes, and requests to enable macros are also common indicators. Users should verify email authenticity before opening attachments.

---

## 4. Define email spoofing and explain its purpose in cyberattacks.

Email spoofing is a technique in which attackers forge the sender's email address to make a message appear as if it came from a trusted source. The primary purpose is to deceive recipients into trusting the email and taking actions such as revealing credentials, downloading malware, or transferring money. Email spoofing is frequently used in phishing campaigns because it increases the likelihood that victims will believe the message is legitimate.

---

## 5. What is Wi-Fi security? Mention two basic methods to secure a wireless network.

Wi-Fi security refers to the measures used to protect wireless networks from unauthorized access, data theft, and cyberattacks. Secure Wi-Fi networks help maintain the confidentiality and integrity of transmitted information. Two basic methods to secure a wireless network are enabling WPA2 or WPA3 encryption and using strong, unique passwords. Additional measures such as changing default router settings and updating firmware further improve wireless network security.

# SECTION B

## 1. A user receives a friend request from an unknown profile that appears genuine. Analyze the risks involved, explain how attackers create fake identities, and describe preventive measures.

Accepting friend requests from unknown individuals can expose users to several cybersecurity and privacy risks. Cybercriminals often create fake profiles using stolen photos, copied personal information, and fabricated backgrounds to appear trustworthy and legitimate.

Once a connection is established, attackers may collect personal information from the victim's profile, conduct social engineering attacks, spread malware through malicious links, or use gathered information for identity theft. They may also gain access to private posts, contact lists, and sensitive information.

To prevent such risks, users should verify the identity of unknown individuals before accepting requests. Privacy settings should be configured to limit profile visibility. Personal information should not be shared publicly, and suspicious messages or links should be avoided. Users should regularly review their friend lists and report fake accounts to the platform.

Practicing caution and maintaining strong privacy controls significantly reduce the risk of social media-based attacks.

## 2. Explain how email-based attacks occur by discussing phishing, spoofing, and malicious attachments. Also explain how users can identify and prevent such attacks.

Email-based attacks are among the most common cyber threats because email remains a primary communication tool for individuals and organizations. Attackers use various techniques to deceive users and compromise systems.

**Phishing** involves sending fraudulent emails that appear to come from legitimate organizations. Victims are tricked into revealing credentials or sensitive information through fake websites.

**Email spoofing** occurs when attackers forge the sender's address to make emails appear trustworthy. This technique increases the likelihood that recipients will believe the message is authentic.

**Malicious attachments** contain malware such as viruses, ransomware, or trojans. When opened, these files can infect devices and compromise data.

Users can identify such attacks by checking sender addresses, examining links before clicking, looking for grammar mistakes, and being cautious of urgent requests. Organizations should implement email filtering systems, spam protection, and security awareness training. Multi-factor authentication and endpoint protection software provide additional layers of defense.

By combining technical controls with user awareness, organizations can significantly reduce the effectiveness of email-based cyberattacks.

## 3. Discuss the importance of securing a home Wi-Fi network by explaining common attacks and best practices to prevent unauthorized access.

Home Wi-Fi networks connect multiple devices including computers, smartphones, smart TVs, and IoT devices. Securing these networks is essential because attackers often target poorly protected wireless connections.

Common attacks include password cracking, where attackers attempt to guess weak Wi-Fi passwords. Evil Twin attacks involve creating fake wireless networks that mimic legitimate ones to steal user credentials. Eavesdropping attacks allow attackers to intercept unencrypted communications transmitted over wireless networks.

Several best practices can improve Wi-Fi security. Users should enable WPA3 or WPA2 encryption and create strong passwords. Default router usernames and passwords should be changed immediately after installation. Router firmware should be updated regularly to address vulnerabilities. Disabling unnecessary features and enabling network monitoring can further enhance protection.

Guest networks can be used for visitors and IoT devices to reduce exposure of primary systems. These measures help prevent unauthorized access and protect personal information from cyber threats.

# SECTION C

## 1. A company is facing repeated email-based cyberattacks leading to data breaches. Design a comprehensive email security strategy including technical controls, user awareness, and monitoring mechanisms.

Email remains one of the most frequently targeted attack vectors in modern organizations. A comprehensive email security strategy must combine technical controls, user education, and continuous monitoring to effectively reduce the risk of email-based attacks.

The first component is implementing **technical security controls**. Advanced email filtering systems should be deployed to detect spam, phishing attempts, and malicious attachments. Authentication technologies such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC should be configured to prevent email spoofing. Endpoint security solutions should scan attachments and links before they reach users. Multi-factor authentication should be enabled for all email accounts.

The second component involves **user awareness and training**. Employees should receive regular cybersecurity awareness training covering phishing identification, social engineering tactics, suspicious attachments, and safe email practices. Simulated phishing campaigns can be conducted to evaluate employee readiness and improve awareness.

The third component is **monitoring and incident response**. Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems should monitor email activity for suspicious behavior. Security teams should investigate alerts promptly and maintain incident response procedures for handling email-related security incidents.

Regular policy reviews, access control management, and secure email gateways further strengthen security. By combining preventive technologies, educated users, and effective monitoring, organizations can significantly reduce the likelihood of successful email attacks and protect sensitive information from compromise.

## 2. Discuss data backup strategies by comparing online and offline backups, explaining causes of data loss, and proposing a complete backup plan for an organization.

Data backups are essential for protecting organizational information against accidental deletion, hardware failure, cyberattacks, and natural disasters. A well-designed backup strategy ensures business continuity and minimizes downtime during recovery.

Data loss can occur due to ransomware attacks, hardware failures, software corruption, human error, power outages, and natural disasters. Organizations must prepare for these risks through effective backup mechanisms.

**Online backups** store data on cloud-based platforms or remote servers. They provide easy accessibility, automatic synchronization, and protection against local disasters. However, they depend on internet connectivity and may be vulnerable if cloud accounts are compromised.

**Offline backups** store data on physical devices such as external hard drives, tapes, or removable media. They are protected from online attacks but require manual management and secure storage.

A complete backup plan should follow the 3-2-1 rule: maintain three copies of data, store them on two different types of media, and keep one copy offsite. Regular automated backups, encryption of backup data, periodic testing of recovery procedures, and access controls should be implemented.

This approach ensures data availability, rapid recovery, and resilience against cyber incidents and system failures.

## 3. A laptop was infected with malware after downloading files from an untrusted source. Explain how malware spreads, prevention techniques, and design a complete endpoint security strategy.

Malware is malicious software designed to disrupt operations, steal information, or gain unauthorized access to systems. Downloading files from untrusted sources is a common method through which malware infects devices.

Malware spreads through infected email attachments, malicious websites, pirated software, removable media, phishing attacks, and software vulnerabilities. Once installed, malware can steal credentials, encrypt files, monitor user activities, or provide attackers with remote access to the system.

Several prevention techniques can reduce infection risks. Users should download software only from trusted sources, keep operating systems updated, avoid suspicious links, and use reputable antivirus software. Multi-factor authentication and regular backups also strengthen security.

A comprehensive endpoint security strategy should include antivirus and anti-malware solutions, host-based firewalls, patch management systems, endpoint detection and response (EDR) tools, application whitelisting, and data encryption. Security policies should restrict unauthorized software installation and enforce strong authentication mechanisms.

Continuous monitoring, employee awareness training, vulnerability assessments, and incident response procedures should also be integrated into the strategy. By combining technical safeguards with user education, organizations can effectively protect endpoints from malware infections and other cyber threats.
